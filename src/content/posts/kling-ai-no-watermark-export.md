## The short answer

There are only two legitimate ways to get a Kling video without a watermark: **pay for a Kling subscription** (Standard from $6.99 for the first month, then $8.80 a month, 660 credits) and **toggle the no-watermark option at export** — because Kling defaults to watermarked downloads even for paid users. There is no free, legal way to remove the watermark from a free-tier export, and third-party "watermark remover" tools violate Kling's terms and degrade the video.

All paid plans include watermark-free exports. The cheapest way in is Standard at $6.99 for the first month — an introductory price; it renews at $8.80 a month. Prices exclude VAT and vary by country, so the checkout total may be higher. Check the official pricing page before you pay.

One caveat on the free tier: several November 2026 reports say the Basic plan no longer includes monthly credits at all, and that the 66-credits-a-day figure may be outdated. Kling's own pages are inconsistent on this point. If you are planning around the free tier, check the live membership page in your account — the number it shows you is the one that counts.

## How to export without a watermark, step by step

**1. Make sure you are on a paid plan.** The no-watermark toggle only appears for paying subscribers. On the free tier, every export is watermarked and there is no toggle to change that.

**2. Generate your video as normal.** The watermark setting is applied at export, not at generation, so you do not need to do anything different while creating the clip.

**3. Open the export or download menu.** After the video finishes generating, open the download options.

**4. Find the watermark toggle and switch it off.** This is the step people miss. Kling defaults to watermarked downloads even for paid users — you have to actively choose the no-watermark option each time you export. Look for a toggle or a checkbox labelled "watermark" in the export panel, and make sure it is off before you download.

**5. Download and check the file.** Open the downloaded video and confirm there is no visible watermark. If there is one, you exported with the toggle still on — re-export with the toggle off. Do not crop the watermark out of a watermarked export; that is a different problem, and it is addressed below.

## The toggle quirk, and why it catches people

The most common complaint from paying Kling users is not the price — it is that they paid and the watermark was still there. The reason is the default: Kling's export panel defaults to watermarked downloads, and the no-watermark option is a toggle you have to find and switch off. Multiple users in 2025 and 2026 reported downloading watermarked videos on a paid plan and only discovering the toggle after searching for it. If you have ever paid for Kling and still got a watermarked video, this is almost certainly why. The toggle is per-export, not a permanent account setting, so you need to check it every time.

## The invisible watermark you cannot remove

There is a second watermark that no toggle removes. Kling embeds Google's SynthID — an invisible watermark — in its video output. SynthID survives screenshots, re-compression, cropping, and re-encoding. It is not visible in the video, and it does not affect playback, but it means the file is detectable as AI-generated by anyone who checks. This is deliberate: it is part of how AI video platforms prove provenance and comply with emerging regulation. Do not buy a tool that promises to remove SynthID — it cannot, and the attempt will cost you money and degrade your video.

## What does NOT work

| Method | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Cropping the watermark out | Cuts the frame, changes the aspect ratio, and looks unprofessional — and the SynthID watermark survives |
| Third-party watermark-remover tools | Violate Kling's terms of service, degrade video quality, and cannot remove SynthID |
| Blurring or covering the watermark | Visible in the video, looks worse than the watermark, and does not remove SynthID |
| Downloading from a screen recording | Captures the watermark along with the video, at lower quality |
| Asking support for a one-off clean export | Support does not provide watermark-free exports to free-tier users |

The only clean path is a paid plan plus the toggle. Everything else is a workaround that costs more — in quality, in time, or in account risk — than the $6.99 first month of Standard.

## Is Standard worth it just for the watermark?

It depends on what the video is for. If the video is a draft, a test, or a personal project, the watermark does not matter — use the free tier and ignore it. If the video goes to a client, a paid ad, or a public channel, a watermark signals "unpaid tool" and undermines the work, and $6.99 for the first month is cheaper than any reshoot. The credit math matters too: Standard's 660 credits a month buys roughly 66 ten-second clips at 720p, so the watermark removal is bundled with a usable amount of generation. If you only need one clean video a month, subscribe for one month, export, and cancel — you keep access until the end of the cycle.
