Search for "Writesonic free plan" and you will find dozens of articles describing a free tier with a monthly word allowance. Most of them are out of date. As of November 2026, Writesonic has no permanent free tier. The old Free and Lite tiers are gone. What exists instead is a free trial — no credit card required — and then paid plans starting at $99 a month. This article explains exactly what you can do before paying, what happens when the trial ends, and how to use the trial so you know whether the paid plans are worth it before the first charge.

## First, what changed: Writesonic is no longer just an AI writer

Understanding the trial requires understanding what Writesonic sells now. The company has repositioned itself as an AI search-visibility platform. Article generation — the feature most people associate with Writesonic — is now a capped module inside each plan, alongside GEO tracking (how often your brand appears in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews), site audits, and agentic workflows.

That repositioning explains the pricing. You are not buying a word allowance; you are buying a bundle in which articles are one component. The Starter plan's 15 AI articles a month looks small next to older word-based plans, because the plan's value is spread across articles, 50 tracked prompts, and 10 site audits. If you only need article generation and nothing else, the bundle is poor value. If you need all three, it starts to make sense. The trial is your chance to find out which of those you actually are — before paying.

## What the free trial actually includes

The official FAQ states the trial gives "full access" with no credit card required. That is the company's own wording, and it is the most specific statement available: the trial is not a feature-limited demo, and you do not hand over payment details to start it.

What the official pages do not state clearly is the trial's length or its exact article allowance. The pricing page's FAQ confirms the no-card trial and the 7-day refund on paid plans, but does not publish a trial duration or a trial article count in the text available. That gap matters, because "full access" without a stated limit could mean full access to everything until a hidden cap, or full access for a fixed number of days. Before relying on the trial for a real publishing deadline, check the trial terms shown at signup — they are the authoritative source, and they may be more specific than the public pricing page.

What you can reasonably expect from the trial, based on the official FAQ and independent reviews from September to November 2026:

- **Access to the article generator.** You can generate AI articles and see the full workflow, from brief to draft, the same way a paying Starter user would.
- **Access to GEO tracking and site audits.** The trial is the only way to judge whether the visibility features are useful for your site, since they have no equivalent in a pure writing tool.
- **Access to the brand voice feature.** Starter includes 1 brand voice; the trial lets you set one up and test whether generated articles sound like you. For a guide to training a brand voice properly, see our [Jasper Brand Voice setup](/posts/jasper-brand-voice-setup/) — the principles (feed it your best writing, keep samples consistent) apply to Writesonic too, even though Jasper gives you 2 voices on Pro and Writesonic gives you 1 on Starter.
- **Trial runs of agentic workflows.** All self-serve plans include only 10 agentic-workflow trial runs, so even paying users get a taste rather than a full workflow product. Treat agentic workflows as a feature to evaluate, not a feature to depend on.

What you should not expect: a permanent free allowance that survives the trial. There is no free tier to fall back to. When the trial ends, access ends unless you pay.

## What happens when the trial ends

This is the part most "free plan" articles get wrong, so here it is plainly:

1. **You choose a paid plan, or you lose access.** There is no free tier to downgrade to. The cheapest option is Starter at $99/mo on monthly billing or $79/mo on annual billing.
2. **Annual billing is paid 12 months upfront.** The $79/mo annual rate saves 20% over monthly billing, but it means paying $948 at once rather than $99 a month. Do not pick annual billing to save money if the upfront payment strains cash flow — the monthly plan exists for exactly that reason.
3. **If you pay and change your mind, there is a 7-day refund.** The official FAQ offers a 7-day, no-questions-asked refund. That effectively extends your evaluation window by a week past the trial — but only if you remember to ask. Set a reminder for day 5 of the paid plan.
4. **Your trial content and settings are the question to ask at signup.** Whether articles, brand voice settings, and tracked prompts created during the trial carry over to a paid plan is the kind of detail that varies by vendor and is not stated on the public pricing page. Check it before you invest hours in trial setup you might lose.

## What Starter actually buys you

If the trial convinces you, Starter is the entry point. Here is what its limits mean in practice:

**15 AI articles a month.** That is roughly one article every two working days. For a solo blogger publishing twice a week, it is enough. For a content team publishing daily, it is not — Basic ($249/mo monthly, $199/mo annual) raises the cap to 25 articles, and Growth ($499/mo monthly, $399/mo annual) to 50. Be honest about your real publishing volume before choosing: the difference between 15 and 25 articles a month is $150 a month, and buying the wrong tier is the most expensive mistake in this pricing table.

**50 tracked prompts.** GEO tracking covers ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews on Starter. Fifty prompts means fifty questions you monitor for brand visibility — enough to track your core topics, not enough to track every long-tail query. Enterprise adds Perplexity, Claude, Grok, DeepSeek, Copilot, Meta AI, and Google AI Mode, for a total of 8 tracked platforms.

**10 site audits a month, 100 pages each.** That is 1,000 pages audited a month. A small site can audit its entire content in one or two audits. A large site cannot — Basic raises audits to 20 a month at 1,200 pages each, and Growth to 50 a month at 2,500 pages each. If your site has more than 1,000 pages, Starter's audit limit will be a constraint from day one.

**1 user, 1 project, 1 brand voice.** This is the limit that surprises teams most. Starter is a single-user plan: one login, one project, one brand voice. You cannot add extra users on Starter — the $50/user/mo add-on is marked unavailable on Starter in the official comparison table, and third-party sources say the add-on is available only on Basic and Growth. Basic allows up to 3 extra users at $50/user/mo; Growth up to 2 extra users at the same rate. (The base seat counts for Basic and Growth beyond these add-on limits are not cleanly confirmed from the official table — check the official site.) If two people need to work in Writesonic, Starter is not a team plan, and the real entry price for a team is Basic at $249/mo plus $50/mo for the second user.

**10 agentic-workflow trial runs.** Every self-serve plan includes the same 10 trial runs. This is not a workflow automation product at these prices; it is a demo of one. Do not buy a plan for the agentic workflows.

## How to use the trial: a 5-step evaluation plan

A trial without a plan wastes itself. You have limited time and unknown limits, so evaluate in this order — most important decision first:

**Step 1 — Generate one article on a topic you already know well (day 1).** Pick a topic you have written about before, so you can judge the output against your own knowledge instead of against nothing. Check three things: factual accuracy, coverage of the points that matter, and whether the draft needs heavy rewriting or light editing. If the draft is wrong about facts you know, no brand voice setting will fix that.

**Step 2 — Set up the brand voice and regenerate (day 1–2).** Feed the brand voice your best existing article on a similar topic, then regenerate the same article and compare. The difference between the two drafts is what the brand voice is worth. If the difference is small, Writesonic's voice feature is not your reason to buy.

**Step 3 — Run a site audit (day 2).** Audit your site and read the findings. Are they actionable and specific to your site, or generic advice you already know? An audit that tells you things you already knew is not worth $79 a month. An audit that finds a real problem — broken internal links, missing pages, thin content — is.

**Step 4 — Set up GEO tracking for your 10 most important prompts (day 2–3).** Ten prompts, not fifty. You want to know whether the tracking data is useful and whether your brand appears at all. If your brand never appears in AI answers for your core topics, the tracking tells you the size of the problem — but it does not fix it, and the fix (content, not tracking) is the real work.

**Step 5 — Decide by the trial's stated end date, not by feeling.** Before the trial ends, write down a yes or no with a reason. "Yes, because the audit found three fixable issues and the article draft needed only light editing" is a decision. "It seemed fine" is not. If the answer is no, let the trial lapse — there is no free tier to preserve, so there is nothing to lose by walking away, and you can return later if the product changes.

## The cost of staying: Starter vs. Basic vs. Growth

| Plan           | Monthly | Annual (per month) | AI articles/mo | Tracked prompts | Site audits/mo   | Extra users               |
| -------------- | ------- | ------------------ | -------------- | --------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Starter**    | $99     | $79                | 15             | 50              | 10 × 100 pages   | None — add-on unavailable |
| **Basic**      | $249    | $199               | 25             | 100             | 20 × 1,200 pages | Up to 3 at $50/user/mo    |
| **Growth**     | $499    | $399               | 50             | 200             | 50 × 2,500 pages | Up to 2 at $50/user/mo    |
| **Enterprise** | Custom  | Custom             | Custom         | 8 platforms     | Custom           | Custom                    |

Cost per article on annual billing: Starter $5.27 per article ($79 ÷ 15), Basic $7.96 ($199 ÷ 25), Growth $7.98 ($399 ÷ 50). Starter is the cheapest per article — but only if you use all 15. If you publish 8 articles a month on Starter, your real cost is $9.88 per article. Unused articles expire at month end with no rollover, so the plan you need is the one whose cap is just above your real volume, not the one with the lowest sticker price.

Growth also offers a +20 articles add-on at $100/mo, which is $5.00 per additional article — slightly cheaper than Starter's per-article rate. If you are on Growth and need a few more articles in a busy month, the add-on is better value than upgrading to Enterprise.

## The free alternatives, honestly compared

If the trial ends and you decide not to pay, here is what free actually buys elsewhere:

- **Grammarly Free ($0, permanent):** grammar, spelling, punctuation, tone detection, and 100 AI prompts a month. It is a writing assistant, not an article generator — it will not draft a 1,500-word article for you. But it is genuinely free, permanently, and for editing and short-form writing it is the best free option in this set. See our [Grammarly vs. Writesonic for content teams](/posts/grammarly-vs-writesonic-content-teams/) comparison for the full picture.
- **Jasper:** no free tier, 7-day trial on Pro ($59/seat/mo annual). Same trial-only model as Writesonic, at a lower entry price for a single seat — but Jasper is a pure writing tool with no GEO tracking or site audits, so the comparison depends on whether you need those.
- **Semrush Content Toolkit:** no free tier, 7-day trial, $60/mo annual with unlimited standard AI articles and 5 SEO-boosted articles a month. If your main need is SEO articles rather than GEO tracking, the toolkit's unlimited standard articles may be better value than Writesonic's 15-article cap — see [Semrush Content Toolkit vs. Jasper](/posts/semrush-content-toolkit-vs-jasper/) and our [SEO blog workflow](/posts/seo-blog-workflow-semrush-grammarly/).
- **Higgsfield Free ($0):** 0 credits, watermarked image-only generations, no commercial use. Not a writing tool at all — relevant only if your content plan includes video, in which case see our [blog-post-to-video-ads workflow](/posts/blog-post-to-video-ads/).

## Limitations / Who Should NOT Buy

- **Do not sign up expecting a free plan.** There is not one. Articles, blog posts, and videos that describe a Writesonic free tier with a monthly word allowance are describing a product that no longer exists. If a free tier is a requirement, Grammarly Free is the honest choice — or a 7-day trial elsewhere.
- **Do not buy Starter for a team.** One user, one project, one brand voice, and no way to add users. A two-person team needs Basic at minimum ($249/mo monthly), and the real cost is $299/mo with the second seat. Price the team before you price the plan.
- **Do not buy Writesonic for article generation alone.** At $5.27–$9.88 per article on Starter's annual rate, depending on how many of the 15 you use, it is expensive for pure drafting. Its value is the bundle — articles plus GEO tracking plus audits. If you only need drafts, Jasper Pro ($59/seat/mo annual) or the Semrush Content Toolkit ($60/mo annual, unlimited standard articles) may be cheaper for the same job.
- **Do not count on rollover.** Monthly article, prompt, and audit allowances expire at month end. A quiet month does not bank capacity for a busy month. If your publishing volume is seasonal, the monthly plan (despite costing 25% more than annual) may waste less than annual billing with expiring allowances.
- **Do not buy for agentic workflows.** Ten trial runs on every self-serve plan is a demonstration, not automation. If workflow automation is the requirement, evaluate it as a separate purchase with its own trial — do not let it influence this decision.
- **Do not trust the trial length or trial article cap from this article.** Neither is published clearly on the public pricing page. Read the terms at signup and treat them as the truth. If the signup screen does not state a limit, assume the trial can end or cap without much warning, and do your evaluation in the first two or three days.
- **Annual billing is a $948 commitment on Starter.** The 20% saving is real, but it is paid 12 months upfront. If you are unsure after the trial, take the monthly plan for one month and use the 7-day refund window as your safety net, not as your evaluation plan.
