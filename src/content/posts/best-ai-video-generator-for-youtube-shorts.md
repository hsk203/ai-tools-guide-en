## Revid: finished Shorts from an idea or a link

Revid turns a prompt, a link, or a trending format into a finished short-form video with a script, visuals, voiceover, and captions, then publishes it to TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube.

- **Price:** paid plans run $32–$199 a month: Growth 2,000 credits, Elite 5,000, Ultra 12,000, annual billing at the low end. Sign-up is free, with free credits and no card required.
- **What a credit buys:** Revid's FAQ puts a typical 30-second video at roughly 40–80 credits, so Growth's 2,000 credits cover around 25–50 such Shorts a month. Monthly credits do not roll over.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** end-to-end workflow, scheduled publishing, voice cloning on higher tiers, and Seedance 2.5 up to 4K on Growth. The catch: the credit meter runs on everything, so daily posting needs the higher plans, and template-driven output is what YouTube's inauthentic-content rule targets.

## InVideo AI: prompt-to-video with a real editor attached

InVideo AI pairs a prompt-driven agent, Agent Two, with a full timeline editor, stock footage, and several video models — Seedance, Kling, and Veo — in one place.

- **Price:** per-seat plans run $10–$150 a month: Starter 400 credits, Plus 2,000, Max 5,000, annual billing at the low end. From InVideo's official pricing page, read on November 18, 2026.
- **Free limits:** InVideo's pricing page advertises no free tier — cancelling drops you to a Free plan — and free exports carry an InVideo watermark. Credits do not roll over.
- **What a credit buys:** the official page prices Seedance 2.5 at roughly 56 credits per 5 seconds at 720p and 96 at 1080p, so Plus's 2,000 credits buy about 20 five-second 1080p clips.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** the timeline editor is on every plan, manual editing costs no credits, and Plus unlocks the full Seedance range at 1080p and 4K. The catch: Starter has no Seedance 2.5 or 2.0 access — only Fast and Mini — so it is a demo, not a workflow.

## OpusClip: your long videos, cut into Shorts

OpusClip does not generate video from nothing: you feed it a long recording, and it picks the moments worth clipping, scores them for virality, reframes them to vertical, and adds animated captions.

- **Price:** plans run $14.50–$29 a month: Starter 150 credits, monthly billing only; Pro 300 credits a month per pack. One credit processes one minute of source video.
- **Free limits:** 60 credits a month, a watermark on exports, and clips stop being exportable after 3 days. Confirmed on OpusClip's pricing page and in seven reviews.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** the virality score helps pick clips, Pro adds a scheduler and posting to nine networks, and Pro credits roll over for two months. The catch: Starter exports 9:16 only with no scheduler, and the meter counts source minutes — a two-hour podcast costs 120 credits before one clip exists.

## Fliki: script-to-video for narrated Shorts

Fliki turns a script into a narrated video, matching stock footage or AI visuals to each line and voicing it with one of more than 2,000 voices.

- **Price:** paid plans run $21–$88 a month: Standard 180 credits, Premium 600. The free plan gives 3 credits a month at 720p with a watermark.
- **What a credit buys:** Fliki bills AI video at 0.1–6 credits per second by model, so 180 credits stretch from 30 minutes of the cheapest generated video to barely a minute of the most expensive.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** the deepest voice library here, voice cloning from Standard, and Series scheduling. The catch: reviewers note Fliki bills credits during editing and previewing, not only on export, and Premium's maximum length is listed as 40 minutes in the plan table but 30 in the FAQ — check inside the app before paying for a year.

## Pictory: blog posts and scripts become video

Pictory turns written material — a script, a URL, a blog post — into a captioned video with stock footage and an AI voiceover.

- **Price:** plans run $19–$199 a month, but treat the entry price with caution. Pictory's own comparison page lists Starter at the low end on annual billing (200 video minutes), Professional in the middle (600 minutes), and Teams at the top end. Other October 2026 checks put Starter at $19–$25 a month annually — the checkout page is the only figure that counts.
- **Free limits:** a 14-day free trial; reports vary between 3 watermarked trial projects and 15 trial video minutes.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** minutes-based billing instead of credits, and Pictory's own pages say AI credits roll over while the subscription is active. The catch: the pricing page is the least transparent here, and reviewers report a strict no-refund policy — test inside the trial before subscribing.

## CapCut: the free editor every Shorts workflow needs

CapCut is a real video editor first and an AI generator second: the free plan gives you the full manual timeline, auto-captions, and 1080p export.

- **Price:** Pro runs $15–$20 a month; the free plan costs nothing and includes the full manual timeline.
- **Free limits:** 1080p export, a limited number of AI auto-edits and auto-caption minutes per month, and no cloud storage on new accounts. Pro adds 1,200 AI credits a month, 1 TB of storage, 4K and HDR export, and the full AI toolkit.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** a project built only from free assets exports with no watermark at 1080p, and auto-captions are fast enough for Shorts. The catch: one Pro-marked effect, filter, transition, sticker, or music track locks the whole export with a watermark. CapCut's terms also require a paid plan for commercial use.

## Google Flow: the cheapest cinematic b-roll

Google Flow is Google's filmmaking tool built around Veo 3.1 and Gemini image models. It generates short cinematic clips from a prompt or a still frame, but it is not a Shorts assembler.

- **Price:** Google AI plans run $4.99–$199.99 a month. Flow itself has no price: every account gets 50 free Flow credits a day, and a Google AI plan adds a monthly block — Plus 200 credits, Pro 1,000, Ultra 10,000–25,000. Google's US list prices.
- **Free limits:** 50 credits a day, never rolling over. A Veo 3.1 Lite clip costs 10 credits, Fast 20, Quality 100 — so the free daily grant buys five Lite clips. Generation may pause for non-subscribers at peak hours; no 1080p upscaling without a paid plan.
- **Strengths and weaknesses:** the best free tier here, and the cheapest path to footage that looks shot rather than generated. The catch: clips run 4–8 seconds, so a 60-second Short needs many generations, and Google says Veo 3.1 Fast, Quality, and Omni Flash 1.1 retire on December 5, 2026.

## The Shorts rules that decide whether you get paid

A generator can make the video. Only YouTube decides whether it earns.

**Shorts can run up to 3 minutes.** YouTube raised the limit from 60 seconds on October 15, 2024 — but Shorts over one minute with claimed music or film clips can be blocked from monetization.

**The Partner Program bar is 1,000 subscribers plus one watch threshold.** Either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days. A lower early tier — 500 subscribers plus 3 million Shorts views or 3,000 watch hours — unlocks fan funding only. Creators keep 45% of their allocated share.

**Mass-produced AI videos do not earn.** On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content", covering repetitive or mass-produced material. The rule does not ban AI tools: it targets videos made from generic templates without the creator's original insight — slideshows with no narrative, the same template with nouns swapped, or AI personas advising on sensitive subjects. A Short with your own script, edit, and one idea per video passes; fifty identical template videos do not.

**Disclose AI content, and know the 2027 change.** When AI generation makes something look real, YouTube asks you to disclose it during upload; disclosing alone will not limit a video's audience or its eligibility to earn. From February 1, 2027, new Partner Program applicants need 20 million Shorts views in 90 days or 8,000 watch hours, and existing partners need 10 million Shorts views per 90 days to keep sharing Shorts ad revenue.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can AI-generated Shorts be monetized on YouTube?**

Yes, if the videos are original. YouTube's July 15, 2025 policy update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" : mass-produced, template-driven AI videos are ineligible for monetization, while AI used to enhance original storytelling is fine.

**What is the cheapest AI video generator for YouTube Shorts?**

Google Flow is the cheapest way in: every account gets 50 free Flow credits a day with no subscription, and a Veo 3.1 Lite clip costs 10 credits. For a finished Short from one prompt, Revid's Growth plan starts the paid ladder, and CapCut's free editor covers assembly and captions.

**How long can a YouTube Short be in 2026?**

Up to 3 minutes. YouTube raised the limit from 60 seconds on October 15, 2024. Keep music licensing in mind: Shorts over one minute that contain claimed content can be blocked from monetization.

**Do AI video generators remove the watermark on the free plan?**

Almost never. Revid, InVideo AI, Fliki, OpusClip, and Runway all watermark free exports or restrict them. CapCut is the exception: a project built only from free assets exports clean at 1080p, but one Pro-marked effect watermarks the whole video.
