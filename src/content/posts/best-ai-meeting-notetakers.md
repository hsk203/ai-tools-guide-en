## Verdict at a Glance

- **Best free plan:** Fathom — unlimited recordings and transcriptions, instant AI summaries, $0 forever
- **Best for live transcription:** Otter — 300 minutes a month free, real-time transcript, 30-minute cap per meeting on the free tier
- **Best for sales teams:** Fireflies — unlimited transcription on the free tier, 400 minutes of storage, CRM integrations, AI scorecards on paid plans
- **Best for private meetings:** Granola — no bot joins your call, notes stay on your device until you share them
- **Best value paid plan:** Otter Pro at $8.33 a month on annual billing, or Fathom Premium at $16 a month

**The short answer:** if you want a free notetaker that does not expire after a trial, Fathom is the one. If you need a live transcript on screen during the call, Otter is the one. If your meetings feed a CRM, Fireflies is the one. And if the idea of a bot sitting in your meeting makes you uncomfortable, Granola is the one. The free tiers here are genuinely usable — which is rare in AI tools — so start free and only pay when you hit a specific limit.

## The free tiers, compared

Free plans are where most notetakers are won or lost, because a meeting notetaker is useless if it runs out of minutes halfway through your week. The four leading tools take very different approaches.

### Fathom

- **Price:** Free $0 forever; Premium $20 a month ($16 annual); Team $19 a month ($15 annual, 2-user minimum); Business $34 a month ($25 annual)
- **Free limits:** unlimited recordings and transcriptions, instant AI summaries, clips, playlists, search across calls
- **What you pay for:** advanced summaries, AI action items, a conversational meeting assistant, a custom meeting bot, CRM field sync (Business), coaching metrics (Business)
- **Platforms:** Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams

### Otter

- **Price:** Basic free; Pro $16.99 a month ($8.33 annual); Business $30 a month ($20 annual); Enterprise custom
- **Free limits:** 300 minutes of transcription a month, 30-minute cap per meeting, 10 recordings a month, 3 people in the Otter AI Chat
- **What you pay for:** 1,200 minutes a month on Pro (6,000 on Business), no per-meeting cap, 30-minute cap removed, 60+ summary templates, CRM and workflow automation
- **Platforms:** Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams

### Fireflies

- **Price:** Free $0; Pro $18 a month ($10 per seat annual); Business $29 a month ($19 per seat annual); Enterprise $39 a month ($35 per seat annual)
- **Free limits:** unlimited transcription, 400 minutes of storage, unlimited team members, AI summaries, search and notes
- **What you pay for:** 2,500 minutes of storage on Pro, 5,000 on Business, custom summaries, AI scorecards, analytics, custom dictionary
- **Platforms:** Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, GoTo, plus a desktop app

### Granola

- **Price:** Free (bot-free notes for 25 meetings); Pro $14 a month; Team $18 a month ($14 annual)
- **Free limits:** 25 meetings on the free tier, then you need Pro
- **What you pay for:** unlimited meetings, team features, custom templates, shared folders
- **Platforms:** Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack huddles, in-person on Mac (with consent)

## First-hand finding: Fathom's free plan has two quirks worth knowing

We read Fathom's official pricing page on 15 November 2026, and two details in the feature table do not appear in most roundups. First, the free plan's bot-free capture — recording without a visible bot joining the call — is a beta feature for Mac only. On Windows, or in a browser, the bot still joins. If the whole reason you picked Fathom was to avoid the bot, that only works on a Mac today. Second, the "Ask Fathom" conversational assistant — the ChatGPT-like interface for asking questions about a call — is marked "Limited use" on the free plan and unlimited only on paid plans, and the table notes a "Max 3 users / domain" limit on some free-tier features. None of these are deal-breakers, but they are the kind of limit you only discover after you have already moved your meetings over. The free plan is still the most generous in this category — unlimited recordings and transcriptions is a claim no competitor matches — but read the feature table before you commit.

## How to choose, step by step

**1. Count your meetings.** If you have more than 300 minutes of meetings a month, Otter's free tier will not last the month — Fathom or Fireflies will. If you have fewer than 300 minutes, Otter's free tier is fine and its live transcription is the best in the category.

**2. Decide how you feel about bots.** A bot that joins your call announces itself to everyone in the meeting. Some clients and some teams dislike it. Granola avoids the problem entirely by taking notes locally without joining. Fathom offers bot-free capture in beta on Mac. If this matters to you, it matters more than price.

**3. Check where the notes go.** If your sales team lives in HubSpot or Salesforce, Fireflies' CRM field sync — writing summary fields back into the CRM automatically — is worth more than any transcript. If you just need a summary in your inbox, Fathom's free summaries are enough.

**4. Try the free tier for two weeks before paying.** Every tool here has a usable free tier except Granola, which caps at 25 meetings. Two weeks of real meetings will tell you more than any review: whether the summary catches your action items, whether the bot annoys your clients, and whether the transcript is accurate on your accent and your jargon.

## What to watch out for

**Per-meeting caps.** Otter's free tier caps each meeting at 30 minutes. If your meetings run an hour, the free tier stops halfway through. Fireflies caps storage at 400 minutes, not per-meeting length — a different kind of limit, and easier to manage.

**Consent and recording laws.** Some jurisdictions require all parties to consent to recording. The tools handle the notification differently — Fathom and Otter announce the bot, Granola asks for consent on in-person recording. The legal responsibility is yours, not the tool's.

**Summaries are not minutes.** AI summaries catch action items and decisions well, and they miss nuance, tone, and anything said off-mic. If a meeting decides something important, read the transcript, not just the summary.

## The bottom line

Start with Fathom. Its free plan — unlimited recordings, unlimited transcriptions, instant summaries — is the most generous in this category, and the paid tiers only matter once you need advanced summaries, action items, or CRM sync. Switch to Otter if you need a live transcript on screen. Switch to Fireflies if your meetings feed a CRM. Switch to Granola if you refuse to put a bot in your calls. The wrong choice is paying for a plan before you have hit a free-tier limit — and on Fathom, that limit is hard to reach.
