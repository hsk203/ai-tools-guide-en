## ElevenLabs, in detail

ElevenLabs is the reference point for the whole category. Its Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo models hold the top two spots on the Artificial Analysis voice-quality board, with Elo scores of 1336 and 1334 — ahead of Google's Gemini 2.5 Pro and every other competitor. It is also the most flexible: 31 languages, 3,000+ voices in the library, instant voice cloning, and an API that other tools build on.

- **Free:** 10,000 credits a month, about 10 minutes of audio, no commercial license, instant voice cloning included
- **Starter:** $6 a month (about $5 on annual billing), 30,000 credits a month, about 30 minutes, commercial license, instant voice cloning
- **Creator:** $22 a month, 121,000 credits a month, about 2 hours, 6 professional voice clones
- **Pro:** $99 a month, 600,000 credits a month, about 10 hours, 30 professional voice clones
- **Scale:** $330 a month, 2,000,000 credits a month, about 33 hours

The credit math is the thing to understand before you buy. ElevenLabs charges roughly 1 credit per character. A 30,000-credit Starter plan is therefore about 30 minutes of audio a month — enough for a few short videos, not for a podcast. A 10-minute video script is roughly 1,500 words, or about 9,000 characters, which is nearly the entire free tier. If you make video ads, budget one Starter plan per two or three videos a month, not per video.

ElevenLabs also has the widest ecosystem: a Voice Design tool that builds a voice from a text description, a Sound Effects API, a Dubbing Studio that translates video into 29 languages, and a Conversational AI platform for voice agents. The free tier includes instant voice cloning, which is unusual — most competitors gate cloning behind a paid plan.

## The alternatives, by job

### Murf — corporate training and e-learning

- **Best for:** training videos, e-learning courses, internal communications
- **Why:** Murf ranks second for training voiceovers in 2026 comparisons, with a library tuned for instructional delivery and a workflow built around scripts and slides rather than single clips
- **Watch out for:** it is not the cheapest per minute, and it is not the best for creative voiceover work

### WellSaid — English corporate narration

- **Best for:** English-language corporate narration, training, and product explainers
- **Why:** WellSaid is English-only, which sounds like a limitation until you hear the output — the voices are tuned for corporate delivery, and the pricing is by downloaded minutes, so you only pay for what you keep
- **Watch out for:** no multilingual support, and the per-minute pricing can surprise you if you iterate a lot before downloading

### Google Gemini TTS — free, and good enough

- **Best for:** trying AI voices without paying, and for personal projects
- **Why:** Gemini's text-to-speech is free, ranks fifth on the voice board, and is built into the Gemini app — no sign-up for a voice tool, no credit card
- **Watch out for:** no commercial licensing clarity for business use, and no voice cloning

### Fish Audio and Cartesia — bulk volume under $0.02 a minute

- **Best for:** generating a lot of audio cheaply — audiobooks, long-form narration, bulk content
- **Why:** both price under $0.02 a minute at volume, which is a fraction of ElevenLabs' per-minute cost on the low tiers
- **Watch out for:** fewer voices, less polished cloning, and smaller communities for troubleshooting

## First-hand finding: the free tier is 10 minutes, not 10,000 credits

The number everyone quotes for ElevenLabs is "10,000 free credits a month", and it sounds generous until you convert it. At roughly 1 credit per character, 10,000 credits is about 10 minutes of audio — one short video, or two short ads. Worse, the free tier has no commercial license, so you cannot legally use those 10 minutes in a paid ad or a client video. The real entry point for commercial use is Starter at $6 a month, which buys about 30 minutes with a commercial license. That is still the cheapest commercial voice in this category, but it is not free — and anyone budgeting a video pipeline on the free tier will run out of minutes and hit a licensing wall in the same week. Count characters, not credits, when you plan.

## What to watch out for

**Play.ht is dead.** It shut down on December 31, 2025. Old roundups still list it, and old affiliate links still point at it. Do not sign up, do not buy a plan, and do not trust any article that recommends it without a date.

**Commercial licenses are not automatic.** ElevenLabs' free tier has no commercial license. Murf and WellSaid have different terms per plan. If the voice goes into a paid ad, a client video, or a product you sell, check the license on the plan you actually pay for — not the plan you read about.

**Voice cloning has a consent problem.** Cloning a real person's voice without their permission is illegal in several jurisdictions, and ElevenLabs requires verification for professional clones. Clone your own voice, or a voice you have written permission for. Do not clone a celebrity, a client, or a colleague as a shortcut.

**Multilingual quality varies.** ElevenLabs supports 31 languages, but quality is not equal across them — English is the strongest, and smaller languages can sound flat. If your audience is not English-speaking, test the specific language before you commit to a plan.
